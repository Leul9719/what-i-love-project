It Started With Curiosity

At first, my interest in phones was pretty simple. I was just curious about them. I wanted to know what made one phone different from another and why people cared so much about the newest releases. But the more I looked, the more there was to discover.

I started consuming more and more tech content, and I realized that there was never just one thing that made a phone interesting. I could spend time looking at camera comparisons, then suddenly find myself learning about processors, software, displays, battery life, design, or some tiny specification I had never even thought about before.

That curiosity kept pulling me back. Every time I thought I understood something, there was something else to learn about. Eventually, I became just as interested in the stories surrounding technology as the technology itself.

I couldn't afford any of it, and my brother used to ask why I even bothered watching if I wasn't going to buy it. He wasn't wrong. But that never stopped me. I just wanted to know.
Not every phone became famous because it was great. Some became famous because something went completely wrong.

Phones that bent in people's pockets. Phones that literally caught fire. Products that were supposed to change the industry but became memorable for completely different reasons.

And that's where things get interesting. This gallery is a collection of some of those moments the strange, funny, surprising, and sometimes disastrous stories that made the phone industry what it is today.

How I Got Hooked

The phone I watched more videos about than I'd like to admit

The Galaxy S21 Ultra was the phone that really caught my attention. I remember seeing it in the thumbnail of one of MKBHD's videos, and before I even watched the review, I thought, "That design is incredible. " When I finally watched the video, MKBHD seemed to appreciate the same things I noticed, which made me even more interested in the phone.

The more I learned about it, the more I liked it. The Galaxy S20 Ultra had impressive specifications, but something about the S21 Ultra felt different. It wasn't just about having powerful specs on paper. The design, cameras, display, performance, and overall experience felt like they finally came together and made sense to me.

I didn't even own the phone, but I found myself watching review after review, comparing different opinions and learning everything I could about it. Looking back, this was probably one of the moments when I realized just how interested I had become in smartphones
